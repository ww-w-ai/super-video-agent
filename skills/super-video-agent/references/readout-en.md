# Read-out — English (`en`)

Read `references/readout.md` first. This file lists what English adds.

## Sounds the spelling shows, and the ones it hides

English spelling does not mark stress, and some spellings stand for two different words.

| Case | Example | In `say` |
|---|---|---|
| Stress moves, noun vs verb | record, present, object, content | the voice picks by sentence position; rewrite only if a take misreads it |
| One spelling, two words | read (now / past, "red"), lead (verb / metal, "led"), live (verb / adjective), tear, wind | if a take picks the wrong one, rephrase the line or respell by sound: `red` |
| Abbreviations | vs., e.g., i.e., etc., Dr., St. | write as said: versus, for example, that is, et cetera, Doctor; `St.` as Street or Saint, by meaning |
| Names the spelling misleads | Worcester, Kubernetes, Nginx | respell by sound: `WUSS-ter`, `koo-ber-NET-eez`, `engine X` |

Silent letters and "the" before a vowel ("thee apple") need nothing; the voice handles them.

## Invented names and homophones

- **An invented name close to a famous one is read, and heard, as the famous one.** A made-up
  shop, product or character name a vowel away from a well-known name drifts to the well-known
  vowel. Respell it in `say` by sound, away from the famous name, and list the line as a point
  for the owner to listen to; a respelling can hold on some takes and not on others.
- **STT cannot separate homophones.** `won` (the currency) and `one`, `four` and `for`, `eight`
  and `ate` come back as the same text, so a clean STT check does not prove which one the voice
  meant, and an invented name heard as a common word is not proof of a misread either
  (`references/voice.md` "Did the voice say the line?"). List such lines for the owner to
  listen to; where the meaning hangs on the word, rephrase within what the source says so it
  cannot be taken the other way.

## Numbers

| Written | Say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `5.5` | five point five | the decimal point is said "point" |
| `Opus 5.5`, `v2.0` | Opus five point five, version two point oh | versions read like decimals; a `0` is usually "oh" |
| `2026`, `1999` | twenty twenty-six, nineteen ninety-nine | years are read as two pairs |
| `2000`, `2005` | two thousand, two thousand five | years 2000–2009 are read as one number |
| `9/10` (date) | September tenth | numeric dates are read differently by different audiences; write the month name |
| `3:05 pm`, `15:20` | three oh five PM, three twenty PM | clock time; a single-digit minute takes "oh" |
| `$16.34` | sixteen dollars and thirty-four cents | the symbol written first is said after the number |
| `$2.5M` | two point five million dollars | same; the scale word comes before the unit |
| `27%` | twenty-seven percent | unit said once, after the number |
| `3–5` (range) | three to five | |
| `3-2` (score) | three to two | not "three minus two" |
| `1st`, `2nd`, `3rd` | first, second, third | ordinal words |
| `1,500` | fifteen hundred, or one thousand five hundred | both are natural; keep one form for the whole film |
| `555-0142` (phone) | five five five, oh one four two | digit by digit, `0` said "oh" |

## Acronyms, names, symbols

| Written | Say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `API`, `STT`, `CLI`, `npm` | A-P-I, S-T-T, C-L-I, N-P-M | said letter by letter |
| `NASA`, `GIF`, `JSON` | NASA, gif, JAY-son | said as a word |
| `SQL` | S-Q-L, or sequel | both are common; follow how the product's own team says it |
| `macOS`, `GitHub` | mac O S, GitHub | the product's own spoken form |
| `xhigh` | x-high | two parts, not one word |
| `x` (size, ratio) | by, times | "1920 by 1080", "three times faster" |
| `/`, `#`, `@`, `&` | or / slash, number / hashtag, at, and | say the word the line means, not the symbol name |

## Quoting a wrong reading

"Say 2026 as twenty twenty-six, not two thousand twenty-six": with `2026` written in both places,
the voice reads it the same way twice. Spell the wrong form out in words:
`say: "Say twenty twenty-six... not two thousand twenty-six."`
